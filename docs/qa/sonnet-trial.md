# Sonnet writer trial (QC)
Question: does a Sonnet-written injury page match the Opus-written pages on the same checks?
Trial pages (written by Sonnet): patellofemoral-pain, patellar-tendinopathy (both knee).
Opus comparison pages: meniscal-tear, pcl-injury, mcl-sprain, fai-syndrome (written by Opus agents).
Checks: (1) validator clean; (2) every reference matches PubMed (authors, title, journal, year, PMID); (3) every quoted number appears in the cited abstract; (4) depth vs the ACL exemplar (rehab stages, special tests, differentials, red flags); (5) animation sheet reads correctly.
Result (QC, 30 Oct): PASS. Sonnet pages are equal to the Opus baseline on every check, at small n.
- Validator: both Sonnet pages clean, 0 warnings.
- References: 40 of 40 Sonnet references exist in PubMed with matching authors, title, journal, volume, pages and DOI. 12 differ only in year (online date vs print year, the standard Vancouver convention). Opus baseline (pcl-injury): 20 of 20 correct, 5 year-convention differences, same pattern.
- Numbers: 72 sentences with a number and a citation across both Sonnet pages. 0 fabricated. An automatic screen flagged 19; all resolved as format differences (CIs written "x to y"), rounding (0.17 to 0.2), per-group values (40% and 49% adherence), values only in PMC full text of the 2016 consensus papers, or the writer's synthesis. One synthesis softened by recommendation: "rises of 20-30 VISA-P points are realistic". Opus baseline: 6 of 20 flagged, same kinds (anatomical ranges attached to a review).
- Evidence labels: matched the 2016 consensus on all main PFP interventions. One label too generous (education "moderate", fixed to "limited").
- Honesty: both writers volunteered a list of claims they could not trace, excluded a retracted RCT, and flagged a 19.3% follow-up caveat.
- Depth: Sonnet pages equal or deeper (20 references each, 8 to 10 differentials, 6 clinical pearls) than Opus pages (15 to 20 references, 6 differentials).
- Animations: both read correctly end to end.
Limits: n = 2. No fabricated figure in 72 cited numbers gives a 95% upper bound near 4%. The number screen checks that a number exists in the source, not that the sentence means what the source means. Clinical-meaning review of every claim stays with the Opus review pass.
Decision: remaining injuries are written by Sonnet; scenes and the final review stay on Opus.

## Full reference audit (QC, 13 Nov 2026), 56 pages
Every PMID on the site was fetched from PubMed and compared field by field with the page's citation (first author, title words, volume, first page, DOI, year with an allowance for online-versus-print year).
- 854 references across 56 pages. 854 of 854 exist in PubMed and match: author, title, journal, volume and pages.
- Three automatic flags were false positives (PubMed stores a forename in the surname field for two authors).
- Number screen: 1564 sentences with a number and a citation, 389 flagged (25%) because the number is not in the cited abstract. Spot review of the highest-flag pages shows the same benign causes as the trial: numbers spelled as words in the abstract ("twenty-eight elbows"), CIs written "x to y", details only in full text or a trial protocol, ranges the writer synthesised, and clinical-convention dosages. No fabricated figure found. A systematic full-text audit of the flagged statistics is the remaining QA task.
